Land and water

About 71% of Earth's surface is covered by water, mostly in the oceans. The other 29% is land. This is why Earth looks blue from space.

Water 71% Land 29%

Inside Earth

Crust

The thin rocky skin we live on. It is about 5 to 10 km thick under the oceans and up to about 70 km thick under big mountain ranges.

Mantle

A thick layer of hot rock about 2,900 km deep. It moves very slowly and drives the motion of the tectonic plates.

Outer core

A layer of liquid iron and nickel. Its movement creates Earth's magnetic field, which shields us from the solar wind.

Inner core

A solid ball of iron and nickel, about 1,220 km in radius. It is as hot as the surface of the Sun but stays solid under huge pressure.

The atmosphere

Air is mostly nitrogen and oxygen. The thin layer of gas around Earth holds in heat, gives us air to breathe and burns up most small space rocks.

Nitrogen 78% Oxygen 21%

The last 1% is argon, carbon dioxide and other gases.

Troposphere

Up to about 12 km. Where we live and where weather happens.

Stratosphere

Up to about 50 km. Holds the ozone layer that blocks harmful UV light.

Mesosphere

Up to about 85 km. Most meteors burn up here.

Thermosphere and beyond

Above 85 km. Space begins at the 100 km Karman line.

How we observe Earth

Satellites circle the planet every day and measure things we cannot see from the ground. Together they give us a long record of how Earth is changing.

Weather

Satellites track clouds, storms and hurricanes so forecasts can warn people early.

Oceans and ice

They measure sea level, sea temperature and how much ice covers the poles.

Land and forests

Landsat satellites have photographed Earth's land since 1972, showing cities growing and forests changing.

Air quality

Instruments detect smoke, dust and gases such as carbon dioxide and ozone.
