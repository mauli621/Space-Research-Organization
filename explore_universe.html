What the universe is made of

The stars, planets and people we can see make up only a small part of the universe. Most of it is dark matter and dark energy, which we can measure but not yet explain.

Dark energy 68% Dark matter 27%

The last 5% is ordinary matter: everything made of atoms.

A short history of everything

  1. 13.8 billion years ago

    The Big Bang. Space, time and energy begin expanding from an extremely hot, dense state.

  2. 380,000 years later

    The universe cools enough for the first atoms to form, and light can travel freely for the first time.

  3. About 100 to 200 million years later

    The first stars light up, and the first galaxies begin to grow.

  4. 4.6 billion years ago

    A cloud of gas and dust collapses and forms the Sun and the planets, including Earth.

  5. Today

    The universe is still expanding, and the expansion is speeding up.

Our solar system

Eight planets orbit the Sun, along with moons, dwarf planets, asteroids and comets.

The Sun

A star holding 99.8% of the solar system's mass. Its surface is about 5,500°C.

Rocky planets

Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars. Small, solid and close to the Sun.

Gas giants

Jupiter and Saturn. Huge worlds of hydrogen and helium with dozens of moons each.

Ice giants

Uranus and Neptune. Cold, distant planets rich in water, ammonia and methane.

Asteroid belt

Millions of rocky bodies between Mars and Jupiter, left over from the early solar system.

Kuiper Belt

A ring of icy bodies beyond Neptune. Pluto is its best-known member.

What to look for in space

Stars

Balls of hot gas that make light by fusing atoms. They range from small red dwarfs to giants hundreds of times wider than the Sun.

Nebulae

Clouds of gas and dust. Some are nurseries where new stars are born, and others are the remains of dead stars.

Galaxies

Huge systems of stars, gas and dust held together by gravity. They can be spiral, elliptical or irregular.

Black holes

Places where gravity is so strong that not even light can escape. Most large galaxies have one at their center.

Exoplanets

Planets orbiting other stars. Thousands have been found, and some may be in the right zone for liquid water.
